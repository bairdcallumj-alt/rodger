Section 03

New Zealand at a Crossroads

Standing at a crossroads: should New Zealand protect the old economy or open itself to a new global future?

Big question: When the old British world was disappearing, what should New Zealand do next?

scroll to explore
The old world

Britain was our main customer

Britain was our main customer

For many years Britain bought large amounts of New Zealand's meat, wool, butter, cheese, and other exports.

The Commonwealth connected many countries

New Zealand was part of a wider network of countries linked to Britain through history, trade, and politics.

Local industries were protected

Rules made it harder for overseas businesses to compete with some New Zealand businesses.

The system felt secure

Many people knew where New Zealand's customers were and what products the country would sell.

Britain Commonwealth Reliable customer Trade Protection

New Zealand had been built for a British world. By the 1980s, that world was disappearing.

Imagine this

A Night Market Challenge

Imagine your class is running a food stall at the Manurewa Intermediate Night Market.

You sell sausages, drinks, and snacks. Every year, the parents from your class buy all of your products.

Because you know they will come, you can plan ahead. You know roughly how much food to buy, how much money you might make, and what products to sell.

Your stall feels secure.

Then something changes

Your old customers disappear

One year, all of those parents decide they are going to buy from other stalls instead.

Suddenly, your class has a problem. You cannot rely on your old customers anymore.

You now need to think about how to attract new customers, whether to change your prices, whether to improve your products, and whether to sell something different.

The world has changed, and your stall must change too.

For many years, Britain was like New Zealand's reliable customer. When that changed, New Zealand had to find new customers, new trading partners, and new ways to bring money into the country.

The trade-off

The old system had strengths and weaknesses

The old system was not simply good or bad. It protected some people, but it also created problems.

Secure Industry Protection Competition Innovation Efficiency

What it gave

  • Local factories
  • Protected jobs
  • More local control
  • Stable industries

What it cost

  • Higher prices
  • Less choice
  • Less competition
  • Less pressure to innovate

                  Roger Douglas's answer

                  Open the country

                  In 1984, Roger Douglas believed New Zealand needed to become more connected to the global economy.

                  His answer was to open New Zealand to more trade, more investment, and more competition.

                  Supporters believed this would make New Zealand stronger, more efficient, and more attractive to the world. Critics believed the costs would fall heavily on workers, local industries, and communities.

                  There Was No Perfect Answer

                  New Zealand was not choosing between a good option and a bad option. It was choosing which trade-offs it was willing to accept.
