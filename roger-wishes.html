Roger and His Wishes

Roger Douglas believed New Zealand needed bold economic changes. Like wishes in a story, each reform promised to solve a problem — but every wish came with a catch.

Big question: Were Roger's wishes worth making?

Every wish has a catch

Read each wish, compare the promise and the catch, then decide whether the reform was worth the cost.

Roger Douglas did not just want small changes. He wanted New Zealand to move quickly from an old protected economy into a modern global economy.

Wish One

I Wish Someone Would Buy Our Businesses

By the 1980s, the New Zealand Government owned many important businesses. These included telecommunications networks, railways, airlines, banks, and other services. Roger Douglas believed that the government should not be running so many businesses. He argued that private companies would operate them more efficiently and that selling them would bring money into the country.

🎁 The Promise

Selling government businesses would bring in money, reduce government debt, and encourage companies to become more efficient. Supporters believed services would improve because businesses would need to compete and satisfy customers.

⚠️ The Catch

Once a business is sold, the government no longer controls it. New owners might reduce staff, close services that are not profitable, or increase prices. Some New Zealanders worried that important national assets could end up owned by overseas companies rather than the public.

📍 Example: KiwiRail

New Zealand's rail network was gradually sold and broken up during the reform era. Over time, parts of the rail system struggled, and the government eventually had to buy much of it back. Today, KiwiRail is once again owned by the government.

Wish Two

I Wish More People Would Invest in New Zealand

Roger Douglas believed New Zealand needed to become more connected to the rest of the world. He wanted overseas investors to see New Zealand as a place where they could confidently do business. Changes such as opening financial markets and allowing the New Zealand dollar to float were designed to attract investment from around the world.

🎁 The Promise

More investment meant more money entering New Zealand. Supporters believed this would help businesses grow, create opportunities, and make New Zealand a more successful trading nation.

⚠️ The Catch

When overseas money comes in, overseas influence can come with it. Some New Zealanders worried that more foreign investment would lead to foreign ownership of important assets, rising property prices, and an economy that became more vulnerable to events happening overseas.

📍 Example: House Prices

Over the decades following the reforms, New Zealand became an attractive place for investment. While many factors influence house prices, critics argue that greater flows of investment into property helped push prices higher. Rising house prices benefited many homeowners but made it harder for younger New Zealanders to buy their first home.

Wish Three

I Wish the Government Would Stop Paying for So Much

For many years the government had supported industries through subsidies and had promised expensive future spending programmes. Roger Douglas believed that some of these commitments were becoming too costly and that the government needed to reduce its role in the economy.

🎁 The Promise

Reducing government spending could lower borrowing, reduce debt, and encourage businesses to stand on their own without government support. Supporters argued that New Zealand would become stronger and more financially sustainable.

⚠️ The Catch

When government support is removed, somebody loses it. Some businesses closed when subsidies ended, some workers lost their jobs, and some communities struggled during the transition. Critics argued that the costs of reform were not shared equally across society.

📍 Example: Farming Subsidies

Farmers had received government support through subsidies. Removing subsidies reduced government spending and pushed farms to stand on their own. Some farmers adapted and became more efficient, while others struggled and some farms closed.

Activities

1

Choose the biggest promise

Which wish had the strongest promise: selling businesses, attracting investment, or reducing government support? Explain why.

2

Choose the biggest catch

Which wish had the most serious catch? Think about job losses, foreign ownership, rising house prices, and communities under pressure.

3

Make a judgement

Were Roger's wishes worth making? Use at least one promise and one catch in your answer.

Were the wishes worth it?
