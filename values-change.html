What Are Economic Values?

Economic values are the beliefs and priorities that guide how people, governments, and businesses make decisions about resources, work, and money.

For example: Some people value job security above all else. Others value getting the lowest price. Both are reasonable, but they lead to different economic choices.

"All economic choices reflect someone's values about what matters most."

Protection

Value: Keep jobs and businesses safe by controlling what comes in or out.

Policy: Limit imports, support local industry, restrict competition.

Who benefits? Workers in protected industries, local businesses.

The catch: Consumers might pay more, technology might lag, and protected businesses might become inefficient.

Freedom & Efficiency

Value: Let the market decide. Less regulation, more competition, lower prices.

Policy: Remove tariffs, allow imports, reduce business controls.

Who benefits? Consumers get choice and lower prices, efficient businesses thrive.

The catch: Some workers lose jobs, smaller local businesses might struggle, inequality might grow.

Neither Value Is "Wrong"

Protection Thinking

  • Values: Stability, fairness, supporting communities
  • Question: Why should workers lose jobs for cheaper goods?
  • Risk: Becomes inefficient and uncompetitive over time

Freedom Thinking

  • Values: Innovation, efficiency, consumer choice
  • Question: Why should consumers pay more to protect inefficient industries?
  • Risk: Some people are left behind without support

Roger Douglas Believed In Freedom & Efficiency

When he became Finance Minister in 1984, he saw New Zealand as over-protected and inefficient. He believed opening the economy to competition would make New Zealand stronger, even though some people would struggle in the transition.
