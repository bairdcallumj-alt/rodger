How beliefs and values can shape economies, schools, and entire countries.

Core idea

From values to action

At Manurewa Intermediate, we have the values of challenge and competition.

Challenge and competition are important for individuals because they encourage people to improve themselves, work hard, and learn how to compete fairly with others.

However, challenge and competition can also be used as mechanisms to drive improvement across entire schools, countries, and economies.

A value becomes powerful when it stops being just a belief and starts shaping what people actually do.

What is a mechanism?

Something that acts

A mechanism is something that acts upon something else.

A hinge is a mechanism because it allows a door to move. Your arms and legs are mechanisms because they help your body move.

Competition can also act as a mechanism. Instead of needing lots of inspectors and constant oversight to force people to improve, competition can encourage people and organisations to improve naturally because they want to succeed.

Business example

Furniture with no competition

Imagine a furniture company with no competition.

If the company has a government contract and nobody else can compete with them, they could produce poor quality furniture, uncomfortable chairs, bad colours, and limited choices.

People would have little ability to demand improvement.

When competition arrives

Businesses must improve

Once several companies compete with each other, businesses must offer better quality, lower prices, more innovation, and better design.

In this case, competition becomes a mechanism that drives improvement.

Reflection

Can Competition Go Too Far?

Competition can be powerful, but it is not perfect. Competition can create stress. Some people may be left behind. It can also create winners and losers.

That is why systems still need fairness, support, and clear rules. Competition can drive improvement, but people also need help to take part properly.

Stress
Too much pressure can make people anxious.
